Time Management

advice
By Unknown
2025-11-16

Do you start the school year with an ambitious schedule to find yourself completely drained and off track by the second week? If you're looking at productive people online and wondering, "Do we even have the same 24 hours?" this article is for you.

Effective time management is the crucial skill that separates high-achieving, well-balanced students from those who feel constantly stressed and overwhelmed.

Here are four essential tips to help you schedule your day, manage your tasks, and get the most out of your 24 hours while maintaining a great work-life balance.

1. Prioritization

The biggest hurdle for students is viewing every task with the same level of importance. This leads to burnout and misplaced effort. You need to learn how to triage your workload.

  • Write Everything Down: As soon as you receive an assignment, write it down—whether in a physical diary or on your phone. Forgetting about a task until the last minute is a major source of stress.
  • Identify the Crucial Tasks: Some homework might take 10-15 minutes, while other assignments, like essays or exam question practice, might require a week or two. Prioritize the latter. These are the tasks that make up your grade or immensely help you practice for your actual exams.
  • Utilize the Eisenhower Matrix: This method categorizes tasks based on importance and urgency, helping you decide what to tackle first.
Importance Urgency Action Example
Important Urgent Do (Immediately) Starting an essay, quick daily math worksheet.
Important Not Urgent Schedule Studying for an exam next month, working on a major project.
Not Important Urgent Delegate Homework you don't have the skill set for yet (seek help from a teacher/tutor).
Not Important Not Urgent Delete (Avoid) Any self-imposed task that isn't a requirement and adds unnecessary stress.

2. Avoid Multitasking for Complex Tasks

While the idea of doing several things at once might sound efficient, it's an "L" (loss) for complicated assignments. Switching between writing an essay and doing maths, for example, breaks your focus and flow. You'll likely finish neither and waste more time overall.

  • Focus on One Task to Completion: Use scheduling to dedicate a block of time to one subject or assignment. Once you start, commit to finishing that task or a defined part of it.
  • Harness Parkinson's Law: This principle states that "work expands to fill the time available for its completion." Use this to your advantage!
    • If you have 30 marks of exam questions, give yourself 40 minutes, not 2 hours.
    • By setting less time than you think you need, you force yourself to focus, avoiding distractions like your phone, and ultimately freeing up time for rest.
  • The Exception: Simple multitasking, like listening to a podcast while traveling on the bus, is perfectly fine. But for deep learning and retention, give the task all your focus.

3. Tackle the Beast: Avoid Procrastination

Procrastination is the real time-waster, leading to guilt and stress. The productive people you see online have the same 24 hours; they just use them without this self-sabotage.

  • Physical Restraint: If you find yourself immediately reaching for your phone upon waking or while studying, create physical barriers. Charge your phone across the room, not next to your bed.
  • Use Time Blocking Apps: Apps like Forest or Flora use negative implications (like killing a virtual tree) when you leave the app to check social media. This helps you stay accountable.
  • Identify the Root Cause:
    • Boredom/Challenge: If you feel too challenged and resort to distraction, try the "break it down" tip below.
    • Burnout: Sometimes, procrastination is your body telling you that you need rest. Don't power through exhaustion.
  • Schedule Rest Time: To combat burnout, give yourself a set block of time every day (e.g., 30 minutes to one hour) to just relax. This space should ideally be separate from your study area (like a living room or outside) to help you switch gears mentally and physically. Using a comfortable, relaxing chair during this time can make a huge difference in recovering from a long day.

4. Break It Down: Make Large Tasks Digestible

A large essay, project, or topic revision can feel overwhelming, leading to the impulse to procrastinate. The key is to transform one intimidating task into several smaller, manageable ones.

  • Start with the Easiest Part: When faced with a big assignment, start with the part you find easiest—maybe the introduction, the first paragraph, or a simple initial outline. Finishing a small task gives you an immediate boost of motivation to continue.
  • Structure Your Revision with Pomodoros: Don't try to go from notes to videos to flashcards to exam questions all in one go. Break revision for a single topic into focused, smaller chunks (like 25-minute Pomodoro sessions):
    1. Blurt/Recall: Start with an active recall technique (like mind mapping or blurting) to see what you already know.
    2. Consolidate: Review your notes/watch a video to fill in the gaps and correct misunderstandings.
    3. Practice: Do specific exam questions on the topic.
    4. Review: Mark your answers and analyze why you got questions wrong.

5. Utilize All Types of Support

Don't be afraid to ask for help. If you're struggling with a concept, approach your teacher. They are there to support you. Discuss difficult topics with classmates; sometimes, explaining something to someone else can solidify your own understanding. There are also numerous online resources, tutorials, and revision guides available. Utilize all avenues of support to enhance your learning.

By breaking down tasks, you organize your efforts, target your weaknesses, and make the overall goal seem much less daunting.
